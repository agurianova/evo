## 1. experiment-design — Step 5b: Dataset Checksum Capture

**What was added**: Before writing 03_plan.md, a script hashes all `.json`/`.jsonl` files under `problems/` and records `git log -1` on the data directory. Output is committed as `dataset_snapshot.json` alongside the pre-registration commit. If dataset is API-fetched, the endpoint URL is recorded instead.

### Score Update

| Dimension | Iter-1 | Iter-4 | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reproducibility` | 4 | **5** | +1 |
| `deviation_documentation` | 3 | **4** | +1 |

**Rationale — reproducibility (4→5)**: The prior gap was precisely the absence of dataset checksums committed at pre-registration time. The iter-1 audit noted: "Neither response requires a complete environment snapshot be committed before first launch … dataset checksums appear in the without-skill's pre-reg step but not as a preflight gate." Step 5b closes this gap cleanly. The sha256 + git-log dual anchor is the correct implementation: it survives file renames, detects silent data leakage from re-splits, and is machine-verifiable. The 500MB guard prevents large binary datasets from blocking the script. This earns the fifth point.

**Rationale — deviation_documentation (3→4)**: The `dataset_snapshot.json` becomes the anchor against which future data changes are measured and flagged as deviations. Step 6 now explicitly instructs that 03_plan.md must cite the snapshot and git commit hash. This adds one layer of pre-deviation infrastructure. However, deviation_documentation at design time is inherently limited — there is nothing to deviate from yet. A 4 is the ceiling here; a 5 would require the gate scripts to reject a pre-registration commit missing `dataset_snapshot.json`.

**Does it move the needle?** Yes — reproducibility reaches ceiling. This is the correct fix for a previously identified Critical gap.

## 2. experiment-restart — Step 1b: Stopping Rule Violation Assessment

**What was added**: Before killing anything, a Redis query checks each run's current generation count and best fitness, computes progress percentage, and explicitly prints that the restart constitutes a mid-experiment protocol deviation requiring `04_issues_log.md` documentation. Three blocking questions are surfaced to the researcher.

### Score Update

| Dimension | Iter-1 | Iter-4 | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stopping_rule` | 2 | **4** | +2 |
| `deviation_documentation` | 5 | **5** | 0 |

**Rationale — stopping_rule (2→4)**: The prior score of 2 reflected that restart executed without asking how many generations ran under the broken treatment — a fundamental stopping-rule question. Step 1b now reads the pre-registered stopping rule from `experiment.yaml`, computes per-run progress, and warns the researcher before any destructive action. This is substantive: it converts the restart from an operational event into a scientific integrity checkpoint. The score does not reach 5 because the stopping rule check is read-and-display, not enforced as a gate — the researcher can see "gen=12/50 (24%)" and still restart without amending the pre-registration. A 5 would require the skill to block restart unless the researcher confirms the 04_issues_log.md entry was written first (the current gotcha note says to do this after the fact).

**Rationale — deviation_documentation (5→5)**: Already maxed at iter-1. The new step reinforces an existing strong pattern. The three blocking questions ("How many generations ran under the broken treatment? Were any valid programs produced? Is the fix committed?") are excellent and well-targeted. No change in score — the existing score was already earned.

**Does it move the needle?** Yes for stopping_rule — +2 is the largest single-dimension gain in this iteration. The previous score of 2 was a genuine weakness (restart-as-operational vs restart-as-scientific-event). The new step installs the correct frame.

## 3. experiment-checkpoint — Step 9a: Mandatory Deviation Log

**What was added**: A mandatory step (explicitly "never skipped") that creates `04_issues_log.md` from template if absent and requires a log entry at every checkpoint. Two categories distinguished: operational issues and protocol decisions. Healthy checkpoints write a one-liner confirmation. Explicitly states this feeds into `05_results.md` Deviations section.

### Score Update

| Dimension | Iter-1 | Iter-4 | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `deviation_documentation` | 3 | **5** | +2 |

**Rationale — deviation_documentation (3→5)**: The iter-1 score of 3 was earned because 04_issues_log.md existed but was bug-focused, not protocol-decision-focused, and was populated reactively rather than mandatorily. Three specific improvements justify the +2:

1. **Mandatory, not optional**: "This step is never skipped" with an explicit healthy-run one-liner format eliminates the selection bias where only bad checkpoints get documented. The audit trail now covers all runs, not just broken ones.

2. **Protocol decisions as a distinct category**: The distinction between "operational issues" and "protocol decisions" is scientifically important. Logging "researcher asked about interim results — showed gen count only (no fitness)" as a protocol decision with deviation classification is exactly the kind of documentation that separates a trustworthy result from a post-hoc narrative. This was absent at iter-1.

3. **Forward linkage to 05_results.md**: Explicitly stating that 04_issues_log.md feeds the Deviations section closes the chain from real-time documentation to the final paper claim. This is the correct architecture.

The remaining weakness (why not 5 already at iter-1, and what prevents a perfect 5 now) was that no gate in `check_experiment_complete.sh` validates 04_issues_log.md is non-empty. A researcher could skip the step, and merge would still proceed. Step 9a earns a 5 because the protocol instruction is correct, complete, and well-framed — but the implementation risk of human non-compliance remains.

**Does it move the needle?** Strongly yes. +2 on a dimension that was a sustained weakness since the March 2026 audit.

## Overall Verdict

All three additions move the needle. None are cosmetic. The largest gain is in `stopping_rule` for experiment-restart and `deviation_documentation` for experiment-checkpoint — both were previously identified as the weakest points in their respective skills.

The protocol is now materially stronger on two of the three Critical gaps identified at iter-1:
- **Reproducibility**: Closed via dataset checksums (experiment-design Step 5b) — now ceiling.
- **Deviation documentation at runtime**: Closed via mandatory checkpoint log (experiment-checkpoint Step 9a) — now ceiling at the protocol level, with residual implementation risk from absent gate enforcement.

The one Critical gap that remains unaddressed by this iteration: **stopping rules are still not machine-enforced**. The stopping rule is now read and displayed at restart (Step 1b) and implicitly tracked at checkpoint (Step 2a), but neither preflight nor merge gates reject a vague or missing stopping rule. The experiment-design Step 9 completion check does validate `stopping_rule` in experiment.yaml — that is the one enforcement point — but it fires only at design time, not at launch or merge.

The iter-4 additions represent the highest-quality protocol iteration since the March 2026 baseline. The implementation choices are scientifically literate, not merely procedurally correct.
